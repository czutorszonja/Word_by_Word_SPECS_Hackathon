Building Word by Word

How the Hungarian-learning café Lens changed stage by stage: from stand-in shapes to a realistic Budapest café with a barista you order from, seated guests, and a learning layer that labels, plays, saves and practises words where you use them.

18 Nov

01

First build

18:06

The café blocked out with simple stand-in shapes and placeholder people, so the learning flow could be tried in the Specs preview before anything was made to look real.

02

The basic flow works

18:41–18:46

Setup panel, a word card that plays the word, phrase cards on the counter for ordering, and the first practice board. All four modes run end to end, in plain dark panels.

03

Realistic rebuild

18:39–21:31

Generated 3D props replace the boxes: ingredients in containers, a red espresso machine, a lounge, a generated server and diners. Still toy-like, which later rounds fix.

04

Automated tests

22:05

LEAF scenarios check each done criterion in the preview. All five pass, and they are re-run after every bigger change from here on.

05

Readable panels

22:18–23:08

A see-through hole in the panels is fixed, the word card gets clear Listen and Save buttons, and Use it live gets a compact view that leaves the server visible.

19 Nov

06

First mode colours

00:06–00:12

Each mode gets its own colour (Learn, Use it live, Practice) and the sandwich bar gets more ingredients. The colours turned out too bright and were softened in the next stage.

07

Bigger café, real faces

00:36–04:52

The room grows, the mode colours soften, and the cartoon people are challenged: rejected attempts (a smeared face, caricatures) on the way to calmer, more realistic faces.

08

The story tour

14:14–14:16

An automated tour follows the storyboard: arrive, point to reveal a word, listen, save, reuse it in a sentence, order, learn the table number, practise.

09

New layout

14:32

The coffee bar becomes service-only and the seating gains a window bar, following a re-layout proposal.

20 Nov

10

Menu, entrance and an audit

10:17

Built the evening before: the Menu (demo) button with a saved-words viewer, counter details, the entrance with NYITVA outside and ZÁRVA inside, and the wall menu board. Then an audit clean-up: one ingredient per container, nothing floating, a clear pastry case with the cake under a dome.

11

Real food

11:00

Toy-looking foods replaced: pepper strips and meatballs in steel pans, spiral kakaós csiga, flaky croissants, pogácsa in a basket.

12

Details

11:25

Planters by the door, glass globe lamps, and a different street or park view in every window.

13

A real barista

12:05

The cartoon server becomes a textured, rigged adult who breathes, gestures, moves his mouth while talking, blinks, keeps eye contact and nods.

21 Nov

14

Full walkaround

17:20

After feedback: real seated guests, the barista in a canvas apron, a hand-lettered chalk menu, bigger phrase boxes, and saved words with a listen icon on each.

22 Nov

15

The whole café

11:42–13:29

Younger guests, the first views of the whole café, street windows you can see into, a gallery wall, and the interface placed so nothing covers the menu board or the phrase.

24 Nov

16

One street outside

13:50

Every window used to show a different place and season. Now the window-bar side looks onto one leafy square and the door side onto one tram street, all on the same sunny late-spring morning, each window from its own angle.
